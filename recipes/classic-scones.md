# Classic Scones
**Tags:** Brunch

<img alt="Classic Scones" src="https://tarasmulticulturaltable.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Irish-Scones-3-of-4.jpg" />

> Light, fluffy, and golden, these classic British scones are wonderfully simple to make. Enjoy them warm with clotted cream and jam for a proper cream tea experience.

## Ingredients of the Scone Dough
* 500 g plain flour (plus extra for dusting)
* 2 rounded tsp baking powder
* 2 heaped tbsp caster sugar
* 1 tsp salt
* 110 g cold unsalted butter, chopped
* 300 ml whole milk
* 1 egg, whisked (for glazing)

## Steps
1. Preheat the oven to 190°C / 375°F.
2. Sieve the flour, baking powder, sugar, and salt together into a large bowl.
3. Transfer the sieved dry ingredients into a food processor. Add the cold chopped butter and process until the mixture resembles fine breadcrumbs. Alternatively, rub the butter in by hand using cold hands.
4. Pour the breadcrumb mixture back into a large bowl and make a well in the centre.
5. Pour the milk into the well and bring the mixture together with a fork until it starts to come together.
6. Switch to your hands and gently bring the dough together — do not overwork it.
7. Generously flour a work surface and a rolling pin. Turn the dough out and gently roll to about 3 cm thick.
8. Using a fluted or plain cutter of about 5 cm (2 inches) in diameter, cut out scones and place them on a baking tray lined with parchment paper.
9. Brush the tops with the whisked egg for a glossy finish.
10. Bake for 15–20 minutes, until risen and golden brown.
11. Remove from the oven and allow to cool slightly on a wire rack. Serve warm.

## Notes
Avoid overworking the dough — the less you handle it, the softer and fluffier the scones will be.

Re-rolling leftover dough is possible, but scones cut from re-rolled dough may turn out slightly tougher.

Placing scones close together on the tray so they touch while baking is perfectly fine — they pull apart easily and may even rise better.

**Serving suggestions:**
* Clotted cream and jam (the classic cream tea)
* Butter only, for a simpler option
* Fold raspberries or other berries into the dough before cutting for a fruity variation

The great Devon vs Cornwall debate: Devon puts cream first, then jam; Cornwall puts jam first, then cream. Both are delicious.
