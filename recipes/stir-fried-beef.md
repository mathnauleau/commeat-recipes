# Stir-Fried Beef with Leeks and Onions
**Tags:** Dinner

> Tender slices of beef and deeply browned alliums are stir-fried with a savory, sweet sauce for a fast and intensely flavorful weeknight meal.

## Ingredients
* 1 lb (450 g) flank steak or other tender-cut beef, trimmed
* 1 large leek
* 1 medium yellow onion
* 2 scallions
* 2 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 tbsp minced fresh ginger
* 2 tbsp soy sauce
* 1 tbsp oyster sauce
* 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine or dry sherry
* 1 tsp cornstarch
* 1 tsp sugar
* ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
* ¼ cup (60 ml) homemade or low-sodium chicken stock
* 2 tbsp neutral oil, divided
* Cooked rice, for serving

## Steps
1. Place the beef on a cutting board and slice it thinly against the grain. In a bowl, combine the beef with 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 teaspoon cornstarch, and 1 tablespoon oil. Toss and set aside while preparing the vegetables.
2. Trim the leek, halve it lengthwise, rinse thoroughly, and cut it into 2-inch (5 cm) pieces. Peel the onion and cut it into wedges. Thinly slice the scallions, keeping the white and green parts separate.
3. Stir together the remaining soy sauce, oyster sauce, Shaoxing wine, sugar, black pepper, and stock. Set the sauce aside.
4. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a large wok or heavy skillet over high heat until lightly smoking. Add the beef in a single layer and cook, undisturbed, for 1 minute. Toss and cook until browned but still slightly underdone, about 1 minute more. Transfer to a plate.
5. Add the leek, onion, scallion whites, garlic, and ginger to the wok. Stir-fry until the vegetables are softened and beginning to char, about 2 to 3 minutes.
6. Return the beef and its juices to the wok. Add the sauce and toss until the beef is cooked through and the sauce coats everything, about 1 minute.
7. Remove from the heat, sprinkle with the scallion greens, and serve immediately with rice.

## Notes
Prep time: 20 minutes. Cooking time: 10 minutes. Servings: 4.

Slice the beef thinly and use a very hot wok or skillet. Cook in batches if necessary so the beef browns rather than steams.

The leek, onion, and scallions should retain some texture; avoid overcooking them after the beef returns to the pan.
